Sausage Fighters – physics‑based couch brawler

A local 1v1 party game where players control springy sausage bodies, pick up unsafe weapons, and try to knock each other’s meat off the arena while the physics engine does its best to sabotage everyone.

Personal project Physics & game design Unity · C# Local multiplayer

Play on itch.io

Overview

Sausage Fighters is a top‑down local 1v1 fighting game built around one core joke: the characters are physically simulated sausages made of rigidbody segments that can literally fall apart mid‑fight.

Each body is a stack of joints that tries to stay upright like a spring. Hits apply forces to specific segments; if the impact is strong enough, joints break and chunks of sausage detach, slide across the level, or fly into the distance while the remaining stump desperately keeps fighting.

My role

  • Designed the core loop for fast, readable 1v1 couch matches built on emergent physics.
  • Implemented the segmented “sausage spine” with configurable joints, upright stabilisation, and per‑segment cutting logic.
  • Built the weapon system (pickup, equipping to the highest surviving segment, and cutting/joint breaking on hit).
  • Created simple prototype arenas and game‑flow systems (win checks, respawning weapons, UI labels).

Design goals

  • Make physics the star. The funniest moments should come from the sausage body trying—and failing—to behave like a normal character controller.
  • Instantly readable from the couch. Two players, clear silhouettes, big reactions when a segment pops off.
  • Low friction. Quick rounds, easy pickups, and simple controls so non‑gamers can enjoy the chaos immediately.
  • Physical loss condition. Win by destroying enough of the opponent’s “spine” rather than draining a health bar.

Process & iteration

The game started as an experiment in making a sausage‑shaped ragdoll that could move using only physics forces. Once that felt good enough, I layered on cutting and weapons, then started tightening readability and control.

  • Prototyped multiple joint setups to find a balance between “wobbly” and “controllable”.
  • Added a system to track which segments are still connected to the root so weapons and win conditions update as pieces fall off.
  • Iterated on weapon handling so only the live body can pick up weapons and the blade always ends up on the highest attached segment.
  • Created arena layout to create different simple scenarios of play.

What I learned

  • How to design around physics instead of fighting it—embracing jank, but putting guardrails around it for fairness.
  • Keeping local multiplayer readable with lots of motion on screen.
  • Building small, focused systems.
